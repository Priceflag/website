Before you discount for Black Friday

A discount comes out of your profit per order, not out of your price. So it takes more extra orders to pay for itself than the percentage on the banner suggests. Here is how many.

Why 25% off needs more than 25% more orders

Every order still costs you the same to make and ship. When you take money off the price, all of it comes out of what is left after that cost. A 25% cut in price can be a much bigger cut in profit per order.

To make the same profit, you need enough extra orders to make up the difference. The thinner your margin, the more you need.

A worked example

A product sells for $40.00 and costs $16.00 a unit. You plan 25% off for Black Friday, so it sells for $30.00.

Profit per order at $40.00
$24.00
Profit per order at $30.00
$14.00
Orders needed for the same profit
71.5% more

The price dropped by a quarter, but profit per order dropped by more than 40%. If you sold 100 of these last month, you need about 172 at the discount just to stand still.

Example numbers. $24.00 ÷ $14.00 is 1.714, so you need 71.43% more orders, shown rounded up to 71.5%.

Check the breakeven before you set the discount

Set the discount from the number of orders you can realistically get, not from what looks good on a banner. If 71.5% more orders sounds unlikely, a smaller discount, or a discount on fewer products, may make you more money.

Roll the discount out in stages

Priceflag can roll a price change out by product, so a bad discount reaches a slice of your catalog first. Every shopper always sees the same price.

  1. 25%of the products you picked
  2. 50%of the products you picked
  3. Allof the products you picked
