What just happened

A Galton board drops beads through a lattice of pegs. Each peg deflects a bead left or right at random, and the beads pile into a bell curve. It is the cleanest demonstration ever built that individual randomness produces a predictable distribution — which is the whole claim Priceflag makes about a price change.

  • 01 Proposal — the price waits in the hopper.
  • 02 Simulation — one bead per simulated future; one peg per market uncertainty. The posterior on elasticity visibly tightens as the trials accumulate.
  • 03 Forecast — the pile is the distribution. The band is its 10th to 90th percentile, which by construction is the published elasticity band.
  • 04 Rollout — the board reclines into a shop floor and real orders arrive in neon, landing inside the forecast as exposure steps 5 → 20 → 50 → 100%.
  • 05 Guardrail — real orders start clearing the floor. Past the third breach the machine runs backwards and the price returns to $48.00.

Standalone harness for sections/pf-sim.js. The module owns its markup and its stylesheet; this page supplies only the fonts, the runway above and below, and the mount / probe buttons. Probe prints the rendered state to the console — which is how this is verified, because screenshots are unreliable here: the document reports itself as hidden.

Form factors

One layout rule, driven by the pinned stage's own aspect ratio rather than by any device guess. Wide-and-short — a landscape phone, a landscape tablet, any desktop window — sets the copy beside the board (.pfm-side). Everything taller stacks the copy above it (.pfm-stack), with the board starting below the measured bottom of the copy rather than at a guessed fraction of the height. A stage under 560px tall additionally gets .pfm-short, which compresses the type without removing anything: all five acts, the pour, the recline, the rollout and the reversal are identical at every size.

The price chip above the hopper has its own reserved band, computed before the board is given a pixel: safeTop → chip → by0, where safeTop already clears the device's safe-area inset and whatever the host page keeps pinned over the top of the stage — the demo bar here, the site nav on the landing page, found by hit-testing the top edge. Resize the window, or rotate the device, and it re-lays out live; a wall-clock watchdog catches size drift even when ResizeObserver is silenced. Nothing in the module calls preventDefault or sets touch-action, so touch scrolling and pinch-zoom stay with the page.
