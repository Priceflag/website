What Priceflag does

Priceflag helps Shopify merchants change prices on purpose. It:

  • forecasts what a price change will probably do, using your own product, pricing and order history;
  • rolls the new price out to a growing share of the products you picked, in the stages you set, so every shopper sees the same price;
  • checks daily orders on the products in a rollout against the forecast range.

If orders fall below that range, the rollout pauses (no more products get the new price), we let you know, and we give you a recommendation, like hold, continue, or put the old price back. You decide what happens next. Nothing is reverted automatically. Priceflag doesn't split traffic and doesn't change the price by visitor.

Auto-changes are opt-in, per collection. By default, Priceflag proposes each price change and you approve it. You can opt in, one collection at a time, to let Priceflag make changes within limits you set. Auto-changes are off by default, and you can turn them off any time.

Putting a price back. Putting the old price back is one action by you. Priceflag is designed to restore the previous price and check it in Shopify, but we don't guarantee it.

Forecasts aren't promises. A forecast is a range with a breakeven, estimated from your store's own order history. It's not a guarantee, not a revenue prediction, and not advice on what to charge. What actually happens depends on seasonality, competitors, inventory, marketing and lots of things Priceflag can't see.

We may add, change or remove features as the product grows. If we materially cut functionality you depend on, we'll give you reasonable notice first.

What automation can and can't promise

Guardrail checks, pause alerts and putting a price back are what merchants lean on most, so here's exactly what they depend on. Any of them can fail. A failure can mean a rollout keeps going, or an old price doesn't come back, when you expected otherwise.

  • Data has to show up. Guardrails are checked against order data from Shopify. If Shopify's API is slow, rate-limited, degraded or late, a drop below the forecast range can go unnoticed until the data catches up.
  • Our systems have to be up. An outage, a bad deploy or a bug on our side can delay or block a pause, an alert, or a price change you asked for.
  • We need write access. If the app is uninstalled, its permissions are revoked, or its access token stops working, we can't write any price at all, including putting an old one back.
  • Small samples are noisy. On a small set of products, a guardrail may fire on normal ups and downs, or miss a real decline, just because too few orders came in. Confidence grows as more of the selection goes live. It's never absolute.
  • Other things change prices too. Bulk edits, other apps, sales channels, discount rules and manual changes can move a price out from under an experiment. Priceflag records what it wrote, not what everything else wrote.

We promise to build these carefully and to tell you when they fail. They're designed to work as described. We don't and can't promise that a drop below the forecast range will always be caught, or that a previous price will always be restored.
